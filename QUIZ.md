## Detailed Explanations for Selected Questions

### Why Question 6 is important:
The word "dust" is a perfect example of a contronym. When you "dust the furniture," you're removing dust. When you "dust a cake," you're adding a fine powder. Context is everything!

### Why Question 15 is key:
This distinguishes graded antonyms from complementary antonyms. With graded antonyms, something can be neither hot nor cold (it can be lukewarm). This intermediate possibility is what makes them "graded."

### Why Question 25 matters:
This highlights the binary nature of complementary antonyms. You can't be "very dead" or "slightly alive" in a literal sense—you're either one or the other. This is different from graded antonyms where you CAN say "very hot" or "slightly warm."

### Why Question 34 is crucial:
This tests understanding that repeating decimals are rational. 0.333... = 1/3, which is a fraction of two integers. Non-terminating decimals can still be rational if they repeat, but non-terminating, non-repeating decimals (like π) are irrational.

### Why Question 39 is significant:
This demonstrates the density property of rational numbers. Between any two rational numbers (say 1/2 and 1/3), you can always find another (like 5/12). In fact, there are infinitely many rational numbers between any two rational numbers!
